## 2. Introduction to Growth Mindset

### Question 2

I understand that, according to Carol Dweck, there are two types of mindsets: fixed and growth. 

* People with a fixed mindset think that intelligence and skills are determined at birth. They often shy away from challenges and feel bad when they make mistakes. They care more about how they look than about how much they learn.

* On the other hand, people with a growth mindset believe that abilities can get better with effort and practice. Because of this belief, they embrace challenges, learn from their mistakes, and use feedback to improve themselves.

## 3. Understanding Internal Locus of Control

### Question 3

**What is Internal Locus of Control?**

In 1998 , a study by professor Claudia M.Muller at Columbia University was done on fifth-grade students. The students solved difficult puzzles and were all told they  did very well. But half of them praised for being smart, while other half were praised for working hard.

Students who received praise for working hard chose more difficult puzzles and found enjoyment in solving them. I believe this occurred because they thought effort leads to success.

This is known as internal locus of control, where a person believes their actions determine the outcome.

Students who were praised for being smart formed an external locus of control. They believed that success relies on talent rather than effort.

In my view, if I think my effort is important, I will remain motivated. But if I attribute my success to luck or talent, I might stop trying.
